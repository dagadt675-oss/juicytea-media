## Script

The United States bought Alaska for about two cents an acre, and the new land does not touch the rest of the country. Canada was already in the way.

But let's start with a cleaner cut.

In 1963, Malaysia joined a peninsula to Borneo. One country. Split by open sea.

Then Brunei. In 1890, Sarawak took Limbang, and Temburong was left on the wrong side.

Next, Oman. The U.A.E. keeps the coast in the middle. Musandam never touches home.

In 1885, Cabinda signed with Portugal on its own. The D.R.C. still sits between it and Angola.

And in 1921, Nakhchivan went to Azerbaijan. Armenia fills the gap. The drive home crosses a border.

So every one of these is the same trick. A neighbor, or a sea, holding the two halves apart.

Which makes the receipt look small. Two cents an acre. Hawaii is the long way around.

Blink, and you are back at the receipt.

Looped out loud: "Blink, and you are back at the receipt. The United States bought Alaska for about two cents an acre…"

156 words. At 190 words per minute that is about 49 seconds of speech. With their sentence gap, about 0.7–0.9 seconds, the read lands near 60–68 seconds. Inside 45–70. If it runs long, drop the Hawaii sentence. Do not drop a country. Do not speed Brian up.

## Beat and camera plan

One spline for the whole film. Moves are 0.7–1.5 seconds, eased in and out, never linear, never a whip, never motion blur. Their snap-zoom timing is the target. Their blur whip is not. If A and B are far apart, the move is still one eased flight of about 1.5 seconds, and the basemap can crossfade during it. Holds drift. A hold longer than a second has to be on purpose, and even then something else on the screen changes. An event every 1.4–2.0 seconds: a year stamp, a fill drawing on, a name, a border pulse, the receipt. Numbers and names ease in over about 7 frames. They do not pop in one frame.

Frame 0 is a flat map, already drifting, not a globe. Water is their slate `#5c7e84`. Land is their tan `#9f9071`. Those are flat fills, not a paper-grain texture. Alaska is already the accent, Canada between it and the lower forty-eight. The last half-second eases into that pose. The frame after the last frame matches frame 0. The last frame is one step before it. The parchment-to-satellite crossfade finishes before that half-second, so the loop does not pop a basemap.

Tour, one direction, no doubling back:

1. Frame 0 through the paradox. Alaska filled. A small yellow "about 2¢" is already in, because the highlight has to land by 1–2 seconds. The camera drifts. No cut.
2. On the pivot, ease toward Malaysia. Crossfade the flat map into Blue Marble on the way. Peninsula and Borneo in one frame. Malaysia in the accent. A thin line across the sea while "open sea" is said. Year `1963` in yellow.
3. Short hop to Brunei. Do not stretch it. Limbang is the gap, in the second color. Temburong keeps Brunei's color. Year `1890`.
4. West to the Gulf. Oman south, Musandam north, the U.A.E. between them.
5. Down the African coast. Cabinda, the D.R.C. strip, then Angola. Year `1885`. Drift along the strip so the gap reads.
6. North to the Caucasus. Nakhchivan, Armenia, mainland Azerbaijan. Year `1921`.
7. On "So," pull back just enough to feel the pattern, then the long eased flight to Alaska for the receipt. Yellow "2¢ / acre". Drift west until Hawaii enters on that sentence. Do not cut to it.
8. "Blink, and you are back at the receipt" is the pull-out to frame 0. Last 0.5 seconds is only the lead-in.

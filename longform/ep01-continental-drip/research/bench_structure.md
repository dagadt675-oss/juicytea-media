# Blueprint for a 10–14 minute script
Target about 1,850 words at 150–160 WPM (ElevenLabs deep male voice). That gives 7 sections plus the finale.

## 0:00–0:60
| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | **Impossible-sounding true fact** with a number and a visual anchor | 18 words max; the visual is already moving (globe zoom) |
| 5–12 s | Two hard specifics (place, number, year), then the brand line "Sounds fake. It's 100% real." | No adjectives without numbers |
| 12–20 s | **Flip the misconception:** "You'd think… But…" or "Everyone assumes X. They don't." | |
| 20–30 s | **Promise = the title question, word for word**, plus the end state ("by the end you'll see why…") | |
| 30–50 s | **Teaser stack:** 3 future beats, each one concrete strange detail left unexplained; the last one is the finale ("And the last one… no human has ever…") | Each teased beat must actually come later |
| 50–60 s | One clause of credibility (a named source: NASA GRACE, USGS, a 2023 *Nature* paper), then a hard cut into section 1 | No "let's dive in", no subscribe ask |

## Section template (75–110 s; the finale gets about 2:30)
1. **0–5 s, label line:** "the X that Y" ("the river of death", "the country bigger than Pluto").
2. **5–40 s:** setup, the core fact, and a scale anchor (two anchors or body scale).
3. **40–60 s, the turn:** "But here's the part that doesn't make sense…" or "Hang on. How do we know that?", followed by evidence (map overlay, satellite image, data chart).
4. **60–80 s:** the payoff or twist.
5. **80–90 s, bridge:** close this question and open the next one in the same breath ("…and that's nothing compared to what's under Antarctica.").

**Pacing rules:**
- A small re-hook every 30–45 s (a "but", a question, or a number reveal).
- A section or level change every 90–120 s.
- A change of visual mode every ~2 min (globe, map, chart, public-domain photo) with a sound hit.
- Call back to the teasers at about 25%, 50% and 75%.

## Escalation ladder
| Position | Section |
|---|---|
| S1 | **2nd-strongest** item: pays off the cold open within 2 min |
| S2–S3 | Scale shocks |
| S4 (~35–40%) | "How do we know?" beat on method and evidence (Cleo 4:01) |
| S5 (~45–50%) | Human stakes / gut-punch (BeyondTheBlue 9:57) |
| S6 (~60–65%) | **"Biggest twist":** reframes an earlier section (Geoff 9:25) |
| S7 (~75–80%) | "But the craziest part…" sensory wow moment (BeyondTheBlue 17:28) |
| Finale (85–97%) | The strongest and longest section; answers the cold-open question |

## Ending (last 30–40 s)
1. Answer the main question.
2. One line on what is still unknown.
3. A wonder line people can quote.
4. A comment prompt of 10 s or less that names 2 items ("Which one sounded the most fake?").
5. End screen pointing to one specific video.

Stop there, with no thank-you ramble.

## Phrases that work (all seen in the transcripts)
- "What if I told you…" / "But that's just the beginning."
- "That number sounds impressive, but it doesn't really mean anything until you visualize it. Imagine…"
- "But, hang on. How do we know that?" / "But, this gets weirder." / "And then, you hit something."
- "But the craziest part is…" / "Even weirder…" / "…the biggest twist in the story."
- "Everyone assumes X. They don't."
- "Number six is literally called Disappointment Island. And once you hear what happened… the name starts to make perfect sense."
- "I won't just make up crazy stories… to get you to watch." Trust used as the hook fits a myth-busting brand.

## What kills retention
- Opening with "hello and welcome to…", or any channel intro longer than one line.
- A subscribe ask before the first payoff (GeoMap 0:43).
- Sponsor or self-promo breaks in the first 3 min (Geoff mentions his other channel at 2:21).
- A flat list where every item gets the same time, or a weak #1 (Map Pack).
- Vague claims like "scientists can't explain" with no specific behind them.
- Making things up. Paintify's views rest on unverifiable claims (an upright-walking chimp found in 2012, radio pulses from the Gamburtsev Mountains in 2013). That breaks our facts-only rule and invites a backlash in the comments.
- Long outros or pleas to the algorithm.
- Teasing an item and never paying it off.

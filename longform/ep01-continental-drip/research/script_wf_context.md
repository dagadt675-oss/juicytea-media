# Production constraints for the script (Myth Juice long-form #1)

Channel: "Myth Juice" (@juicytea-l6l) — faceless, US/English audience. Brand: mind-blowing map/geography facts and myth-busting — "sounds fake but is 100% true". Tagline idea: "The world isn't what your map told you."
Narrator: ElevenLabs deep male US voice ("Brian"), ~2.6–2.8 words/s (≈155–165 wpm). No presenter on camera.

## Hard rules
- NEVER mention AI anywhere (script, on-screen, title, description).
- Facts only. Every claim must trace to a primary/reputable source in the facts ledger. Hedge where science is uncertain; never invent a single cause. Label model scenarios (future supercontinents) as scenarios.
- Numbers: US units first (miles, °F, sq mi), metric only as a secondary on-screen label when useful. In the SPOKEN text write numbers as words the way a narrator says them ("sixty-eight percent", "about one in seven", "eighteen seventy-five"), never digits/symbols.
- No "hello and welcome", no channel intro, no subscribe ask before the first big payoff, never "finally / to wrap up / in conclusion".
- No burned-in subtitles; on-screen text is sparse: max 2 text elements, ≤ 7 words each (labels, numbers, chapter cards).

## Length & structure
- Target 2,250–2,500 spoken words (≈ 15–16 min; the brief says ~16:00). No padding: every 10-second window must add new information or a new visual.
- Cold open (0–30 s) that works as a standalone Short; promise by 0:30; first full payoff before 2:00; first jaw-drop ~3:00; re-engage every 2–3 min; stair-step escalation; biggest payoff last; abrupt satisfying ending + cliffhanger bridge to "Part 2".
- First subscribe ask right after the first big payoff (~35–45 % in), one sentence naming future value; second ask over the end screen (last ~15 s).
- 4–6 chapters, titled as questions (no spoilers).
- Comment prompt (commitment): ask viewers something they can answer in one word.

## Humor & animation (owner explicitly wants it: "not dry, funny scenes, animation")
- A recurring animated 3D character: "the Explorer" (low-poly bearded adventurer with backpack). Available animation clips: Idle, Idle_Neutral, Walk, Run, Run_Back, Run_Left, Run_Right, Wave, Roll, Death (comic faint/collapse), HitRecieve, HitRecieve_2 (recoils as if hit by a fact), Interact (reaches/touches), Punch_Left/Right, Kick_Left/Right. Also cute Kenney mini characters (idle, walk, sprint, jump, fall, crouch, sit, die, pick-up, emote-yes, emote-no, interact) and a humanoid library (swim, dance, sit/talk, climb, zombie walk, "No" head shake, "Yes" nod, talking on phone, lay down, slide, sprint/panic run, jump).
- The Explorer can stand/walk on a tiny 3D Earth (Little-Prince style), or appear in small 3D dioramas. Use him for 6–10 short comic beats (2–6 s each): reactions, physical gags (e.g. trying to "catch" a dripping continent, fainting at a number, walking off the edge of a flat map, shaking his head "no" at a myth). Gags must support the point, never replace it.
- Dry, witty narration lines (1 every ~60–90 s), self-aware jokes, absurd-but-true comparisons. No cringe memes, no emojis.
- Sound-design gags are available (record scratch, crickets, boing, cartoon fall whistle) — use sparingly.

## Visual engine capabilities (write visual beats the engine can do)
- Photoreal 3D globe (NASA Blue Marble + bathymetry, DEM relief, night lights, atmosphere), any camera path (space → country), tilt/heading, day/night terminator.
- Highlight any country/region (Natural Earth) in hero-orange with white draw-on outline; dim the rest; labels with leader lines; glowing great-circle arcs; pulsing markers; drag a shape across the globe at true size; rotate the globe upside-down (south-up view).
- Data visuals computed from real data: e.g. land vs ocean antipode overlay, hemisphere splits, counters rolling to numbers, bar charts, stat cards, chapter cards, kinetic headlines.
- Public-domain photos (NASA, NOAA, USGS, Wikimedia PD/CC) with Ken Burns; historic maps/engravings/portraits from Wikimedia (PD) for history beats (e.g. Francis Bacon portrait, 1875 book pages).
- Plate reconstructions (continents moving back in time) can be animated by rotating Natural Earth polygons along published Euler poles / GPlates-style keyframes (approximate, labelled "simplified").
- The animated Explorer character scenes (see above).
- NOT possible: real video footage of people, AI video, copyrighted clips (no Vsauce/Hank Green footage — may mention them by name only if needed).

## !!! OWNER OVERRIDE (latest instruction — takes precedence over the brief and checklist where they conflict) !!!
1. STORYTELLING FIRST. The video is a story, not a lecture or a list of facts: characters (Bacon, Green, Wegener, the 1973 joker, Holden), a mystery, suspense, reversals, emotional beats (Wegener dying on the ice), and the Explorer character's journey as the viewer's stand-in. Every section must move the story forward ("and then…", "but…", "so…"); facts are revealed as plot points, not recited.
2. FEW NUMBERS. Use only a handful of big, instantly graspable numbers in the whole video (about 6–10 total), each turned into a vivid image ("two-thirds of all land", "almost nine in ten people"). Prefer comparisons and images over figures. No decimals, no stacked statistics, no number in every sentence. On-screen counters only for the few hero numbers.
3. NO SOURCES IN THE VIDEO. Never say "according to…", "a 2022 study in…", journal names, "NASA data shows", "computed from Natural Earth", or "every number is computed on screen". No on-screen citations or credits (credits go in the YouTube description only). Historical people may be named as story characters (Bacon, Wegener, Green, Holden), but do not cite papers. Keep the internal facts ledger accurate; it just stays off-screen.
4. Keep everything true; when the science is uncertain, say it in story terms ("nobody has a single answer", "here's what most geologists think").

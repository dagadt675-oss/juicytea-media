# Retention + subscribe checklist (research agent, Nov 2026) — condensed
Evidence tags: [O] YouTube official, [D] study/data, [P] top-creator practice, [F] folklore (A/B test).

Core: YouTube optimizes "valued watchtime" = time rated 4–5★ in satisfaction surveys [O 2021, Beaupré Sep 2026]. Tricks that make viewers feel cheated backfire. TV is #1 US screen [O 2025] → design for 55" TV and phone; depth/production rewarded.

A. First 30 s: frame 1 = thumbnail image in motion (YouTube "Intro" metric = % still watching at 0:30 checks match to packaging) [O]. Line 1 states the myth as viewer believes it; line 2 breaks it with visual proof (refutation beats exposition — Muller 2008) [D]. No logo/intro/subscribe ask. One "wow" visual by ~0:20. End first 30 s on the promise. First 30 s should work as a standalone Short [P].
B. Min 1–3: stakes (why it matters in real life), promise the structure ("each more X than the last" — stair-stepping, biggest payoff last) [P]; show don't announce; fully pay off item #1 before 2:00; flash the final reveal ~1 s w/o explanation; first jaw-drop reveal ~3:00.
C. Mid-video: re-engage ~6:00 then every 2–3 min; no section > 3 min without format change [P]. End each segment on the question the next answers (Ovsiankina effect holds, Zeigarnik memory effect failed 2025 meta-analysis) — close every loop. Meaningful visual change every 5–10 s, full scene change every 20–40 s [F]; don't stack fast cuts over dense explanation (Lang 1999 overload) [D]. Music bed changes per chapter; vary TTS pace/pitch; sound hit per reveal; ~1 s silence before big reveals. Chapters: 00:00 first, ≥3, ≥10 s, titled as questions (no spoilers) [O]. Never signal the end ("finally", "to wrap up", "in conclusion") [P].
D. Ending: biggest item last → one-line "so what" → cliffhanger bridge → cut (abrupt satisfying ending) [P]. End screen last 5–20 s: one specific next video + subscribe; visuals keep moving. Spoken pointer works alone. Have video #2 / playlist live.
E. Subscribe: first ask right after first big payoff (~35–45% in), one sentence, picture moving; second over end screen. Name the future value: "Every week, Myth Juice takes one thing you were taught about the world and checks it against the map. Want the next one? Subscribe." Say "subscribe" clearly (button-glow test) [O]. Brand kit: tagline, recurring verdict stamp (JUICE CHECK: TRUE/FALSE/IT'S COMPLICATED), short audio logo, fixed palette + globe style; numbered series, name #2 on screen.
F. Packaging alignment: lock title+thumbnail before script; thumbnail visual by 0:05, prove title claim by 1:00, first full payoff by 2:00–3:00.
G. Click-away causes: intros, mismatch with thumbnail, static frames >10 s, monotone TTS / mispronounced names, factual errors, spoiler chapter titles, padding. Inauthentic/templated mass content policy (Jul 2025) — make it bespoke.
H. Pre-publish tests: cold read; boring-second audit (no 10-s window w/o new info AND new visual); shot timestamp audit (gap > 10 s); loop ledger (open/close); mute test + audio-only test; thumbnail at 120 px next to RealLifeLore/Johnny Harris + 5-s recall; after publish Test & Compare (3 thumbs, judged on watch-time share).
I. Targets (15–20 min doc): 0:30 ≥ 60–70 %; 1:00 ≥ 55–60 %; AVD ≥ 35 % (stretch 45 %); flat curve after min 3, no dip > 5 pts; subs/1000 views ≥ 4.6 (stretch 7.7); end-screen CTR ≥ 5 %.
J. Psychological levers (honest versions): thumbnail — faceless is fine (faces neutral, 300K-video study), one impossible scale contrast, unresolved visual question (circled region / "?"), ≤ 3 words, high contrast, real data only. Title — curiosity peaks when viewers almost know (Loewenstein); negative words +2.3 % CTR each (Upworthy RCT), use "wrong / missing / never", no fear-mongering. Hook — motion in first 1–2 s (orienting response), identity ("If you grew up with a classroom world map…"); no fake "only 3% know". Mid — small, small, BIG reveal escalation; cliffhanger before each chapter boundary; plant a detail in the hook, pay it off at the end. Subscribe — reciprocity after biggest payoff; commitment via comment prompt ("Comment which one you already knew"); unfinished business (tease #2).

# ep02 terrain data: sources and licences

Every height grid and colour texture in this folder is baked from the sources below by `engine/www/lab/hawaii/tools/region_bake.py` (base bake) and `engine/www/lab/hawaii/tools/detail_bake.py` (close-up detail, 2026-11-09), driven by `../tools/regions_spec.json` and rebuilt with `../tools/rebuild_regions.sh`. The baked files are git-ignored; each region's `meta.json` (committed) lists the exact datasets, scenes, quarter quads and dates it used, and `regions.json` its resolutions (`res`). Nothing here is a photo of another channel, a stock clip or generated imagery.

Credits belong in the video description only (owner rule: no sources on screen). Suggested line for ep02: "Elevation: USGS 3D Elevation Program (3DEP), NOAA NCEI (ETOPO 2022, coastal DEMs), Mapzen Terrain Tiles. Imagery: USDA NAIP (via NOAA Digital Coast), NASA HLS, MODIS and Blue Marble, USGS Landsat, contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2022-2025."

Vertical datums: 3DEP in Hawai'i is local mean sea level, the NCEI coastal grids mean high water (about 0.3 m higher); detail_bake.py takes the lidar on land only (above 0.5 m) and feathers it into the NCEI grid at the coast, so the sea floor keeps the NCEI bathymetry.

Known faults in the 3DEP data and what the bake does about them (2026-11-15):
- Over parts of the open sea the ImageServer returns repeated copies of a land tile (fake cliffs tens to hundreds of metres high: a 5 km strip off Halape). detail_bake.py keeps 3DEP land only as pieces that touch the base bake's land and lie within 60 m of it, and stops with an error if the land area moves by over 3 % or the heights by over 150 m (p99.9) against the base.
- The same fault off south Molokaʻi (molokai_east, 2026-11-15): at least 7 copies of one 125 x 233-texel land tile (up to 610 m high) stacked about 3-12 km off the south-west shore along about 157.09° W, 20.99-21.07° N; the land guard drops them (4.5 % of the 3DEP land texels; the land area stays 32.5 % of the grid).
- A 10 m patch over a 78 m parent (molokai_east in mauinui): with the default bilinear base the parent's texel grid survives in the drained seabed as straight creases that the patch's own finer normals light up (up to 4 m off the surface the parent is shaded with). The spec opt-in `patch.base_interp: "bspline"` takes the parent as the stage shades it (cubic B-spline approximation of its texels): the patch seabed then matches the parent's drawn seabed within 0.03 m, so the edge feather has nothing to hide.
- HI_NOAAMauiOahu_2020_B20, the only 1 m source over Nu'uanu, holds a NW-SE band of interpolated, terraced ground (about 10 m detail) across the valley, the Pali Lookout included, with 3-9 m steps along its two straight edges (a dark line in the Pali ground shot). Those two lines are written in the spec (pali and pali_lookout `detail.height.seams`); the bake tracks the step along them and heals it (each side's trend carried to the seam, half the step ramped out over 60 m on each side, the groove bridged), leaving cliffs over 12 m and rough ground alone. The band is marked in each region's `res.height_interp`, so the texel audit counts its height source as about 10 m there. No finer public height data covers it (the 3DEP catalogue lists only this 1 m project and the 10 m and 30 m seamless DEMs there, checked 2026-11-09).
- Where a region's base colour has no data on land (HLS never clear there, filled black: holei's east cliffs, parts of the Halape shore), the bake takes the tone from the aerial photo instead, in the grade's median hue.
- Height steps (2026-11-15): the shared 16-bit height code spans -8000..+17000 m (0.26 m a step), which shows as contour terraces on the 1-2 m lidar grids under a low sun (the Halape cove ground, ch1 1787). detail_bake.py packs every region whose heights it re-bakes into its own range (meta.json `hmin` / `hrange`, rounded out to 10 m: 0.01-0.05 m a step); make_index.py copies the range into regions.json and hterrain.js / ch3.js decode with it (default -8000 / 17000 where a meta has none, so the other regions are unchanged).
- Winter shadow and cloud in the photo (2026-11-15): where the NAIP photo is far darker or brighter than the summer HLS reference at about 30 m (the Pali's Ko'olau cliffs in deep shadow on 15 Dec 2022, small clouds), the detail transfer fades the photo out (its ratio to the reference must lie within about 0.42-2.4). That ground takes the colour of the kept ground around it (normalised convolution at 45, 135 or 400 m), not the HLS reference's own: the HLS base of O'ahu has rounded, lighter cloud-fill patches with straight edges exactly on those cliffs (a pastel veil under the Pali Lookout in ch2 frame 2889 until this fill). Share of land filled so: pali 5.5 %, pali_lookout 3.9 %, holei 2.4 %, waimea 2.4 %, halape 1.4 %, kilauea_coast 0.8 %, maunakea 0.2 %, halape_cove 0 %. Each re-baked region ships `cres.png` (quarter size, log2-coded, `v = (log2(res_m) + 2) * 25`), the colour's real resolution per texel: the photo's where it was kept, the reference's where it was not, and at least 7 m where the final colour has under about 3 % local contrast at the 3-8 m scale (smooth fill). hterrain.js reads it and lets the procedural detail texturing (canopy, rock, soil) fill that ground instead of a blurred photo. Share of land marked smooth: pali 40 %, pali_lookout 16 %, holei 42 %, halape 85 %, halape_cove 41 %, waimea 36 %, maunakea 83 %, kilauea_coast 90 % (bare lava and cinder at the 3-8 m scale reads as smooth too).
- Known residual: the 2022-23 NAIP photo has thin orographic cloud wisps along the Ko'olau crest south of the lookout (a lighter, hazy north-south strip in pali_lookout's colour, under the agreement limit, so kept); not in frame in s026-s031 as framed on 2026-11-15.

How the aerial and Sentinel-2 colour is matched to the existing grade (detail_bake.py, "detail transfer"): the region's own graded colour stays the tone and the colour at its own scale (HLS 30 m), the imagery only adds what is finer (its luminance detail as a ratio to its local mean, plus its residual colour detail times one chroma factor, after one luminance gain). A winter photo's baked cliff shadow (Pali NAIP of 15 Dec 2022: 8.6 % of the land far darker than the summer reference at 30 m) and clouds bigger than 30 m take the reference's tone; the photo's own hue never replaces the grade.

pali_lookout's colour is refined a second time (`engine/www/lab/hawaii/tools/colour_refine.py`, 2026-11-15): detail_bake.py holds it at 4096 texels (1.44 m), so the same NAIP quarter quads are read again at their native 0.6 m and the detail transfer is repeated one scale finer on a 7628 x 8192 grid (0.72 m; 8192 is SwiftShader's texture limit): the 1.44 m bake keeps the tone and colour at 3 m and above, the photo gives only the finer detail. No new source.

oahu_windward (2026-11-15, `engine/www/lab/hawaii/tools/oahu_patch.py`): a nested patch over the whole of O'ahu in oahu_nuuanu for the views from 5-70 km (s030-s041, ch4). Heights: the NCEI 1/3" topo-bathy grid oahu_nuuanu already uses, at 17 m on land and within 250 m of the coast, faded to oahu_nuuanu's own heights by 800 m offshore (the drained sea floor is unchanged: at most 1 m beyond 800 m). Colour: oahu_nuuanu's HLS colour stays the tone at 100 m and above; 62 NAIP quarter quads (Nov 2021 - Jan 2023) read at their 4.8 m overview add the finer detail on land by the same detail transfer; the sea keeps oahu_nuuanu's colour. The NCEI grid fills ground it had no source for with exactly 0 m (Kailua town, Kailua and south Kane'ohe Bays, Pearl Harbor's lochs) and, in Kailua town, with flat blocks just below 0 m; the same NAIP quads decide (NDWI from their green and near-infrared bands, water above 0.5): the fill that is not water, and those blocks where they join land, become low land at 1 m. Kailua's real ground (about 2-5 m) is not in the data. No new source.

The Big Island hero colour (bigisland, 2026-11-16, `engine/www/lab/hawaii/tools/hls_hero.py` + `hero_refine.py`): the legacy hero colour (hawaii_bake.py: MODIS 250 m / Blue Marble at 4096 texels, 115 m) is re-baked in place at 8192 texels (57.5 x 53 m; 8192 is SwiftShader's texture limit) for the drained-massif and comparison shots (s044-s072, s084-s089, and the kilauea_sf stack behind its patch) and frame 1. On Big Island land (box 156.10-154.78° W, 18.88-20.30° N) a NASA HLS v2 composite adds the finer detail by the same detail transfer: the old colour stays the tone and the colour at 250 m and above, so the grade stays and frame 1 keeps its look at thumbnail size (masked SSIM against final_ep02_1.jpg 0.946 before, 0.944 after; at full size the Mauna Loa lava flows now show as darker tongues). The composite is the per-texel median of the Fmask-clear looks of 44 passes (HLSS30 and HLSL30, May-September 2023-2025; 312 scenes read through the Planetary Computer data API, nothing stored), taken lowest cloud first, then only passes that add a look where the windward cloud left fewer than 6: land never clear 0.7 %, under 3 looks 4.7 %, median 17 looks. Fmask flags fresh dark basalt as water (two thirds of the Mauna Loa summit flows on one 2025 scene), so its water bit is trusted only within about 200 m of the DEM coast. Land with about one clear look fades to the kept neighbours' colour, and land never seen clear takes it. Maui Nui, O'ahu and the sea on this layer keep the old colour (finer regions cover them). The 4096 base is kept beside it as `hero_color_base.jpg` (the reference for a re-run). No new source.

everest (2026-11-16, `engine/www/lab/hawaii/tools/s2_refine.py`, spec "baker": "s2_refine.py"): the colour is re-baked in place at 2048 x 2010 texels (7.2 m) for the s064 summit gag. Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A from the Planetary Computer, 10 April-10 June 2019-2025 (the same pre-monsoon window as the HLS grade; the 18 passes that cleared were 2022-2025): one observation per date (reprocessed duplicates and overlapping tiles of one pass merged), the +1000 offset keyed on each item's processing baseline, scenes chosen by an SCL screen until every pixel has 12 clear passes, the per-pixel median of the SCL-clear ones (never clear 0.41 %). The display curve is the HLS one without its 0.70 knee (sunlit snow is 0.75-0.95 reflectance; the knee would leave no snow detail to transfer). Same detail transfer as detail_bake.py: the region's HLS grade stays the tone and the colour at 30 m, S2 adds only the finer detail; where the HLS grade is much darker than S2 at 30 m (its black holes and baked shadow, 0.5 % of land) the reference takes the S2 tone in the grade's own hue first. cres.png: 10 m where the imagery is kept, 30 m at the 200 m edge fade. Heights unchanged (see below).

## Not used, and why

- NAIP does not cover Midway, Kure, Lalo or the other NW Hawaiian Islands. The National Map's product API lists no High Resolution Orthoimagery dataset and its NAIP ImageServer ends at about 24.5° N (no Hawai'i); the USGS Imagery Only basemap tiles stop at zoom 16 (about 2 m) over Hawai'i (checked 2026-11-09).
- NOAA's 2021 NW Hawaiian Islands topo-bathy lidar DEM (NGS_NWHawaiianIslands_Topobathy_2021_10191) covers only French Frigate Shoals (Lalo): a candidate for a later Lalo / East Island close-up, not used yet.
- The NCEI 6" Hawai'i grid (hawaii_6_mllw_2005): gridding artefacts and track striping (PLAYBOOK_production section 15).
- Finer heights for Everest (checked 2026-11-16 over the everest box): no open DEM finer than 30 m covers it. Copernicus GLO-30 (ESA/Copernicus DEM licence, free with attribution) peaks at 8,738 m in the same cell as the live Terrarium grid and is the smoothest; JAXA AW3D30 and NASADEM peak 8,768 / 8,781 m but 60-80 m off the live summit cell (the paper climber would stand off the top). The live Terrarium z14 heights (SRTM 30 m) stay.
- Esri World Imagery: not public domain (its terms must be checked before monetised use); not used for terrain textures.

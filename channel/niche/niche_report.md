# Myth Juice: is the niche too saturated? Strategy and positioning

Date: 2026-11-08. Author: channel strategist. Inputs: the competition map (99 probes, 1,606 videos, 155 channel pages, 3 vidIQ calls), 8 niche measurements (about 600 probes in total), 3 judges (growth, defensibility, 2-year sustainability), 2 stand-out analyses (title, thumbnail and opening audit; viewer-trust and comment study), the ep02 brief and context, and 47 fresh probes run for this report (files in `research/niche/strat/`).

---

## The short answer

**No. The niche is not saturated, so do not narrow the topic. Two slices of it are saturated (the drain template and the regional template farms), so narrow the format instead: one recognisable signature series, published at low volume.**

- **The niche is healthy.** In the last 12 months, 134 English faceless long-form videos on maps, geography and earth topics reached 100K+ views, from 82 different channels. Demand at the top is still rising: RealLifeLore's median went from 1.0M to 1.3M, and Astrum Earth's from 1.15M to 1.45M.
- **The drain slice is a clone race.** It saw 96 uploads from 50 channels in about 35 days. The median upload is 448 views.
- **Narrowing the topic made channels worse in every case we measured.** Channels that cover only drains have medians of 46 to 7.8K views; region-only channels sit at 1.1K-2K.
- **Narrowing the format made channels better.** Eight small channels that each own one signature format got a median of **99,000 views** across 75 recent uploads. Fifty-nine clone and template channels got a median of **2,650** across 988 uploads.
- **All three judges reached the same verdict on their own:** keep a broad umbrella and run one signature series. Each judge measured with a different lens and each crowned a different winning sub-niche.

**Positioning:** *"Below the Postcard."* Every film takes one famous place you think you know and strips away whatever hides it on the real 3D Earth: water, ice or time. You see its true shape, and you hear the true story underneath.

**Ep02:** keep the topic and change the packaging. Launch as "Hawaii Is Only the Summit. We Measured the Rest." with a postcard-on-the-mountain thumbnail, and ship by about Nov 20. The 56,000-ft payoff is still unclaimed. Every planned element that reads as drain clone number 51 goes.

## 3. Recommended positioning

### 3.1 The channel promise (one sentence)

> **Every Myth Juice film takes one famous place you know from the postcard, strips away what hides it on the real Earth (the water, the ice or the time), and shows you its true shape and the true story underneath.**

The postcard is the myth; the real Earth is the juice. That makes the channel name visible.

### 3.2 The series: "Below the Postcard"

- **Name check:** probed today. "below the postcard" returns 0 on-topic results (lofi music and postcard crafts). "true scale", "waterline" and "pull the plug" are already taken.
- **One episode means:**
  - one famous named place (US in at least 2 of every 3 films);
  - one reveal tool on real data;
  - one true human story (at most 3 named people);
  - one unspent number that lands at 85-90% of the runtime.
- **Rotate the reveal tool.** In each block of 6 films: 2 drains or ice-offs, 1 sea-level or ancient-lake rewind, 1 before/after scar, 1 true-size or cut-away proof, and 1 deep-time or true place story. Never post two drains in a row. The drain is a tool, never the identity.
- **Length:** 11-15 minutes. Spectacle winners run 11-18 minutes; place stories 19-25; Myth Juice's own data supports about 13.
- **Cadence:** 2 long-forms a month, at most 3 when a hot window opens, and never more than 4.
  - Every small channel that holds a baseline posts 10 or fewer a month.
  - The 15-30-a-month farms sit at 1-8K.
  - Low volume also keeps us clear of YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" rule.
- **The recognisable part is the visual device, not a title template:** the postcard, the same camera move and one accent colour. Fixed title templates get cloned and decay. The "geography" channel's "X Isn't Really a Lake. It's a Wound." got 271-291K on its first uploads and 4.6-9K a month later.
- **Place selection gate.** Run it before scripting and again 24 hours before upload.
  1. **Host test:** at least 3 long-forms at 500K+ on the place in the last 12 months, at least 1 of them under 4 months old. Suggested traffic comes from the place's fame, not the lane. Obscure places such as Monterey Canyon, Bonneville and Florida's Ice Age coast had 0 recent hosts.
  2. **Angle test:** no 2 well-made 1M+ videos already show the same reveal.
  3. **Clone test:** no drain or template upload of the same place in the last 14 days. If one exists, change the angle.
  4. **Truth test:** the answer fits one unhedged true sentence.
  5. **Ad and tone test:** no death-toll-led disasters, no graphic war graves.

### 3.3 What we stop doing

1. **Myth lists and abstract myths.** No "N geography facts" lists, and no myth without one named place. Ep01's abstract myth got 1.97% Suggested CTR, and myth-framed uploads have a median of 536.
2. **The drain verb in titles.** No "We Drained X..." (median 448, 14+ channels), no "What X Looks Like Without Ice" (9 copies), no "Hidden at the Bottom". The drain words go in the first spoken line, the description and the tags, never in the title.
3. **The clone thumbnail kit.** No black caption box with an accent word, no curved arrow, no depth label, no tan drained basin, no Everest cross-section and no before/after split.
   - Ep01's thumbnail used the template-farm device set.
   - The brief's packaging rule P5 (white text on a black box) is retired by this data.
4. **Low-poly characters.** They are replaced by paper cut-outs of real public-domain photos, and the cut-out tourist becomes a recurring character.
5. **News chasing** (UN map, Nepal) and **death-toll-led disasters**.
6. **Volume tactics.** No daily uploads, no sleep docs, no 20-48-minute padding.
7. **Farm vocabulary in titles and thumbnails:** hidden, hiding, nobody, secret, found, discovered, bottom, beneath, shocking, terrifying.
8. **Topic narrowing.** No drain-only, US-only or islands-only channel.
9. **AI claims either way.** Never mention AI, and never write "No AI".

### 3.5 How the 7-Shorts-per-video rule fits

Keep 7 Shorts per long-form (14 a month at 2 films). OWNER RULE (2026-11-08, overrides this report's earlier draft): all 7 are full-value Shorts REBUILT from the long-form's own elements (scenes, terrain, characters, facts), never clips cut out of the film, and they need not look or play like the film. Each has a strong hook, a strong payoff and a seamless loop ending. The long-form is never changed for the Shorts.

| Type | Per film | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Funnel Shorts** | 7 | Rebuilt from the film's elements with new vertical compositions and new narration. Related Video set to the long-form. Each is one reveal plus one open question. Never spend the film's payoff early, and never use a spent hook ("taller than Everest" 17M, "hidden islands" 14M, "Hawaii is bigger than you think" 14M). |
| **Idea bank** | - | Proven 1M+ map-trivia Short premises (Detroit is north of Canada 5.4M, Alaska is the easternmost state 4.6M, sea level isn't level 4.4M, Reno is west of Los Angeles) can inspire a Short only when the film's own elements support it; 63 of the 139 pooled ideas already have a 1M+ Short. |
| **All 7** | | Open and close on the same postcard device and sound motif, so viewers learn the brand mark before they meet the long-form. |

Expectations, from the data:

- Shorts are the fastest subscriber engine for small map channels. Mapvix has 80K subs from 96 Shorts and grew 76% in 30 days.
- They are a weak feeder of long-form views. In vendor data, only 2% of channels get noticeable long-form traffic from Related Shorts (Spotter). An arXiv study of 250 creators found long-form views declined after creators started posting Shorts.
- **Measurement rule:** at day 14 of each film, read the Related Shorts share of long-form views.
  - If it stays under 2% after 3 films, keep 7 Shorts as a subscriber engine but cut the funnel cut-downs to 2 and move that time into long-form packaging.
  - If a standalone Short passes 1M, its topic becomes a long-form candidate, subject to the selection gate.

## 4. How we stand out next to the competitors

### 4.1 Title language

**What the data says.** We compared 1,254 clone and template titles with 148 titles from small craft channels.

| Title feature | Clone titles | Craft titles |
|---|---|---|
| hidden / hiding | 19% | 1% |
| nobody / no one / never | 19% | 3% |
| found / discovered / revealed | 12% | 5% |
| bottom / beneath / under | 9% | 2% |
| drain | 4% | 0% |
| Questions | 12% | 35% |
| "Every" | 4% | 19% |
| Median length | 60 characters / 10 words | 47 characters / 8 words |

Each craft channel repeats one frame, such as "Why Can't You Visit X?" or "How Long You'd Last in Every X".

**Our rules (pass/fail):**

- 50 characters or fewer, ideally 30-48.
- The proper noun in the first 3 words.
- Plain-claim or question form.
- This case-insensitive regex finds **no match**: `\b(hidden|hiding|hides|nobody|no one|secret|found|discovered|reveal\w*|bottom|beneath|drain\w*|without (water|ice)|what if|here'?s what|talks about|sitting on|isn'?t really|no longer makes sense|survives|shocking|terrifying|sleep|documentary)\b`
- A probe of the exact title 24 hours before upload finds no on-topic video with the same first 4 words.
- The grammar changes from film to film. For example: claim (ep02), plain fact (ep03), myth check (ep04), question (ep12).
- The title promises only what the film shows.
- Search and host words (drained, Mauna Kea) go in the first spoken sentence, the first line of the description and the tags. That keeps us matched to the host cluster without looking like a clone.

### 4.2 Thumbnail signature: the postcard on the real Earth

**What the data says.** We coded 106 competitor thumbnails.

- **Drain clones (24):** 18 use a dark caption box with an accent word, 17 a curved arrow, 17 a negative-depth label, and 18 an AI-style tan basin. 13 use box, arrow and depth together. On average 21% of their pixels are tan, against 2-11% in every other group.
- **Template farms (14):** 13 use a red/orange highlighted region and 13 a caption box.
- **Craft winners (21):** only 2 use caption boxes. Each keeps its own visual grammar.
- **0 of 106 use a postcard frame or a paper border.**

**The signature:**

- An off-white paper postcard (about #F6F1E4), tilted 3-6 degrees, 30-45% of the frame width, in the upper third.
- The card shows the place's familiar view. Its only text is the place name in large postcard type, at least 18% of the frame height, with a small Myth Juice stamp in the corner.
- Behind and below the card, the real 3D shape from our engine (vertical exaggeration 1.0) fills at least 50% of the frame. Where water is involved, add one luminous cyan waterline.
- Colour comes from light and real materials: dark basalt, cream reef, green island top, pale sky. Never the generic tan sand ramp.

**Ban list:** dark caption box or bar, curved arrow, depth or number label, saturated red/orange highlight, AI face, tan drained basin, Everest side-by-side or cross-section, left/right before/after split.

**Gates**, in `research/niche/work/`:

- `gate.py`:
  - **Thresholds:** at most 10% tan, at most 3% red, at most 45% blue, and a mean feature distance of at least 11.0 to both the clone set and the farm set.
  - **Validation:** it passes 0 of 24 clones, 0 of 14 farms and 9 of 21 craft winners. Both mocks on the tan ramp fail it.
- `feedstrip.py`: a dark-mode strip at 390x220 and 168x94 beside the 9 live neighbours. Pass when 3 of 3 viewers find ours in 2 seconds or less and can read the place name at 168x94.

### 4.3 Series format

"Below the Postcard #N" is a playlist and a pinned-comment label, not an on-screen title card.

- **Every episode has the same skeleton:** postcard drop, then the first real-Earth reveal by 0:30, then the human story by 0:45-0:60, then one reveal per chapter (5-6 chapters), then the payoff at 85-90%. It ends on the payoff line plus a spoken hand-off to the next postcard.
- **Every episode varies:** the place, the story, the reveal tool and the title grammar. Those vary on purpose (policy safety and clone resistance).
- **Why a series identity survives copying:**
  - Fossil's format was copied by channels that got 4.58M and 3.56M, yet Fossil's next video still did 429K.
  - Paintify was copied by Glore (1.59M), yet its parts 2 and 3 did 1.29M and 446K.
  - Template channels' other videos get 145-2.7K.

| 0:00 | Frame 1 = the thumbnail. The first line has 10 words or fewer, with the place in the first 3 words. No logo, title card or sting. |
| 0:02-0:06 | The postcard leaves the frame with the same 1.5-2 s paper-flap sound motif every episode. It tips into the water for drains, peels away for rewinds, and falls open for cut-aways. The real Earth starts changing. |

Throughout: sound-off safe (the card, moving water or terrain, one label at a time); something moves every second; the camera stays at regional zoom. Frame 1 must match the thumbnail (SSIM 0.8 or higher at 320x180).

### 4.5 Trust standards

**What the data says.** We pulled 4,560 top comments from 58 videos.

- Both groups comment at the same rate (0.62 vs 0.65 per 1K views). What differs is what people say:
  - complaints about the synthetic voice: 4.3% of clone comments vs 1.2% of craft comments;
  - pronunciation complaints: 2.8% vs 0.6%;
  - loyalty statements: 0.6% vs 1.8%.
- **What turns viewers against a channel is a visible mistake, not the voice.** Videos where 5% or more of comments flag a concrete error average 14.5% voice complaints; the rest average 2.3% (r = 0.48).
- **Examples:**
  - Origins of Everyday Things labelled the Atlantic as the Pacific, and those comments took 83% of all likes.
  - Below The Map's 124K Hawaii video: 31% of comments, holding 47% of the likes, attack its Hawaiian pronunciation.
  - Unseen Works' accurate, lean 1.75M video drew forgiving comments: "For an AI video it's surprisingly well done".

**Our standards:**

- **Data and places**
  - Real data only, recorded in a per-shot dataset manifest. Vertical exaggeration 1.0 in any height comparison. No generated imagery of real places.
  - Place match: every named place is on screen and labelled within ±1 s of being said (a transcript-vs-shot diff with 0 mismatches).
- **Names**
  - Zero mispronunciations: a lexicon with a verified source for every proper noun, plus a full phone-speaker listen before upload.
  - Native spellings with diacritics in every label and in the description.
- **Claims and packaging**
  - A fact ledger with 0 open VERIFY items at script lock. Absolute words (first, only, never, largest) each need a ledger ID.
  - Every superlative shows what it is measured from (a base line and the same scale, in the same frame).
  - Honest packaging: the promise is partly paid by 0:30 and fully paid by the end.
  - Familiar US units first (Everest, Manhattan, football fields); metric in the description.
- **Writing and sound**
  - Sentences of 15 words or fewer, a banned-phrase list ("delve", "nestled", "let's dive in"...) and no padding. Apply the "I'll save you 15 minutes" test: the one-line answer must not make the film skippable.
  - Loudness of -14 LUFS, narration at least 12 LU over the music, no SFX motif more than 3 times, no unplanned silence.
- **Disclosure and corrections**
  - Never mention AI, never claim "No AI", and answer YouTube's altered/synthetic-content question honestly.
  - Sources are listed in the description. Reply to corrections within 48 h and pin a correction note within 72 h.

### 4.6 Community mechanics

- **Pinned comment at publish**, live within 5 minutes:
  - one question answerable in one tap about the place (Contour Atlas's "which lake is closest to you" was answered in 36 of its top 100 comments);
  - one opinion question ("would you live on a sinking island?");
  - a **next-place vote** between 3 ready candidates that all pass the selection gate.
- **Places with living memory** turn comment sections into communities: 46 of the top 100 comments on the 4.4M Iwo Jima video are veteran and family stories. Expect this for Midway, the Bering crossing and Lituya. Heart those comments and reply to them.
- **Requests feed the topic pipeline.** "Now do Greenland" got 65 likes on the Antarctica video. Every request still goes through the host test.
- **The recurring paper cut-out tourist** appears 2-4 times per film, never near a death or a grave. Keep him if viewers mention him unprompted in at least 2 of the first 4 films.
- **Respect earns trust in sensitive places.** Hawaiian commenters are active on every Hawaii video sampled. Mauna Kea's summit is never a punchline ("Protect Mauna Kea" 2.75M; "WE ARE MAUNA KEA" 1.37M).

## 5. What it means for ep02, "Hawaii Is Only the Tip. We Drained the Ocean."

### 5.1 Decision: KEEP the topic, ADJUST the packaging and the opening

**Why keep it:**

- It is still the best-placed film in the lane. Today's probe "hawaii drained ocean" finds **no Hawaii-only drain long-form above 10K**:
  - Bright Side Dark Secrets' 105-minute sleep doc has 9,046.
  - Unseen Works' Pacific-wide drain has 120K at 4 days and is mostly about manganese nodules, judging from its comments.
- Two new Hawaii drain uploads appeared in the last 12-14 hours: Shape Of America (2-3 views) and WATERLINE (1-6 views). The exact-premise lane is no longer empty, but nobody in it has traction.
- Across the 12 on-topic Hawaii geology or drain long-forms of the last ~90 days, the median is about 355 views. The only two above 10K are the template-farm "Hawaii Is Hiding Something" (124K and 78K).
- **Nobody uses our payoff**, the 56,000 ft measured from the dented base. Shape Of America uses 33,500 ft from the seafloor.
- The host cluster is large and alive: RealLifeLore Hawaii 7.07M, Signore Galilei 3.04M, Be Smart 3.64M. The top-liked comments on Galilei's video ask for exactly our beats: the Ice Age -400 ft (2.1K likes), the landslides on the seafloor map (680), Lō'ihi (558), the Midway albatross (537).

**Why adjust it:** the drain format is saturating fast (96 uploads from 50 channels in 35 days, median 448). The brief's title, thumbnail arms and opening all use grammar this lane already holds. If we ship as planned, the card reads as clone number 51.

## 7. How we will know: 30 / 60 / 90 days after ep02 goes live

Ep01's baselines: Suggested CTR 1.97%, overall CTR 2.27%, about 5.2 s watch time per impression, mobile average view percentage 14.1%, Browse/Home at 4% of impressions (34 of 925), 0 comments, +0 subs.

**Day 30 (ep02 and ep03 published, about 14 Shorts)**

- **Packaging:**
  - ep02 Suggested CTR **4% or more** (target 5%) on 10K+ impressions;
  - **15 s or more watch time per impression**;
  - 0:30 retention **70% or more**;
  - mobile average view percentage **30% or more**.
- **Reach:** ep02 at **10K+ views**. This is the growth judge's commit gate: Suggested CTR of 5% or more and 10K+ views means commit to the series.
- **Trust:** pronunciation, wrong-place and voice complaints **under 2% of comments**; at least 20 replies to the pinned question; every correction answered within 48 h.
- **Shorts:** read the Related Shorts share at day 14.
- **If ep02 stays under 2K views:** fix the packaging (a new thumbnail composition and the title swap), not the niche.

**Day 60 (4 films)**

- **Format, not lottery:** ep03's first-7-day views are **50% or more of ep02's**. The clones' follow-ups got 0.1-5% of their hit.
- **Second-to-fourth films:** median 7-day views **5,000 or more**, beating the niche's typical sub-100K channel at 2,000-3,500. At least one film reaches 50K.
- **Recognition:** Browse plus Home reach **15% or more** of long-form impressions (ep01: 4%). Returning viewers are **10% or more** of views on ep03-ep04.
- **Subscribers:** **300+**, at **3 or more per 1K views**.

**Day 90 (6 films, about 40 Shorts)**

- **Channel median** (long-forms 7-90 days old): **10K or more**, above the mass producers' top quartile of 9.7K. Stretch: **30.7K**, the low-volume craft top quartile.
- **At least 1 film at 100K+.** Good small channels hit 100K on 20-67% of uploads; 1 in 6 is 17%.
- **US share of watch time:** 40% or more (ep01: 43%).
- **YPP:** the 10K median implies roughly 5,500 public watch hours across 6 films (6 x 10K views x about 5.5 minutes), enough for the 4,000-hour bar. 1,000 subscribers is the stretch goal.

**Decision rules**

| Signal | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| CTR under 3% after 2 films that passed the feed-strip test | The thumbnail signature is wrong | Test a new composition. Do not change topic. |
| CTR 5% or more, but mobile average view percentage under 25% | Execution: opening and density | Fix the edit, not the positioning. |
| A hit, then the next film gets under 10% of it | The lottery pattern | Tighten the series identity and hand-offs, and keep cadence. |
| A 90-day median under 3K across 6 films with packaging tests passed | The positioning failed | Switch the signature to the islands one-place story format, the strongest growth-lane alternative (judge 1: 72). Still do not narrow the topic. |
